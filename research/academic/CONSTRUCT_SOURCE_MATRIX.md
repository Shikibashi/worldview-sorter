# Academic construct-to-item matrix

These are original candidate operationalizations, not published validated subscales. Counts are engineering coverage requirements, not reliability evidence.

## ME09: Human-life grounding of moral standards
Whether requirements of human life and flourishing ground objective moral standards. Separate from mere biological description or current preferences. [acad-rand](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ayn-rand/); [acad-egoism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/egoism/); [acad-stirner](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/max-stirner/)

Type: affinity. Evidence items: MEI100@1, MEI101@1, MEI102@1.
Do not infer: moral realism entails a human-nature ethic; atheism entails objective egoism.

- MEI100: Facts about what human beings need to live and flourish can ground objective standards of good and bad action.
- MEI101: There can be a correct account of a good human life that does not change merely because a person wants something else.
- MEI102: A description of human nature does not, by itself, give every individual a moral purpose they ought to fulfill.

## ME10: Choice-to-live conditionality of moral reasons
Whether moral requirements are conditional on choosing to live. This is not identical to making morality relative to any passing desire. [acad-rand](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ayn-rand/); [acad-egoism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/egoism/)

Type: affinity. Evidence items: MEI103@1, MEI104@1, MEI105@1.
Do not infer: denying categorical reasons rules out Rand; hypothetical reasons imply cultural relativism.

- MEI103: The requirements of living well bind a person conditionally on that person choosing to live.
- MEI104: Choosing to continue living can make some actions objectively required, even when those actions conflict with current preferences.
- MEI105: A person must preserve their life as a moral duty regardless of whether they choose life as an end.

## NE15: Ethical egoism as a normative principle
The universal moral prescription to prioritize one’s own good; not a claim about actual motives or a permission for personal projects. [acad-egoism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/egoism/); [acad-rand](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ayn-rand/)

Type: affinity. Evidence items: NEI100@1, NEI101@1, NEI102@1.
Do not infer: self-priority permission entails ethical egoism; ethical egoism entails exploiting others.

- NEI100: Each person morally ought to treat their own long-term flourishing as the ultimate end of their actions.
- NEI101: Even when people choose different lives, morality should direct each of them toward their own good as their final aim.
- NEI102: Concern for oneself can be permissible without being the ultimate moral standard that everyone ought to follow.

## NE16: Rational egoism about practical reasons
Whether one’s own welfare supplies the ultimate standard of practical rationality; distinct from moral rightness and descriptive motivation. [acad-egoism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/egoism/)

Type: affinity. Evidence items: NEI103@1, NEI104@1, NEI105@1.
Do not infer: rational egoism equals psychological egoism.

- NEI103: What a person ultimately has most reason to do is whatever best promotes that person’s own long-term good.
- NEI104: If an action would genuinely make someone worse off overall, that person cannot have decisive practical reason to choose it.
- NEI105: A person can have decisive reason to act for someone else’s sake even when their own life goes worse as a result.

## NE17: Ownness and revisable allegiance to ideals
Original operationalization of non-subjection to fixed ideals and revisable appropriation of projects. An interpretive proposal, not a validated Stirner scale. [acad-stirner](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/max-stirner/)

Type: affinity. Evidence items: NEI106@1, NEI107@1, NEI108@1.
Do not infer: ownness entails compulsive selfishness; ownness entails inability to love; ownness dictates a specific economic institution.

- NEI106: Even an ideal I value deeply should remain something I can reconsider, rather than an authority above me.
- NEI107: I can make a cause my own without accepting that I must serve it regardless of what I later come to value.
- NEI108: Once I recognize an ideal as my true calling, serving it should take priority over whether I still make it my own.

## NE18: Promissory self-binding
Whether a voluntary promise itself creates a defeasible reason beyond current desire; not an exceptionless duty or legal remedy rule. [acad-promises](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/promises/)

Type: monopolar. Evidence items: NEI109@1, NEI110@1, NEI111@1.
Do not infer: promissory reasons are always overriding; legal enforceability proves moral duty.

- NEI109: A freely made promise can give me a reason to act even after I no longer want to perform it.
- NEI110: Assume nobody has relied on a promise yet. The act of promising can still create a moral reason to keep it.
- NEI111: Once my preferences change, the mere fact that I previously promised supplies no remaining moral reason to perform.

## NE19: Continuing endorsement and exit from commitments
Whether continued association or service requires renewed endorsement; separate from duties to repair induced reliance and from valid waiver. [acad-stirner](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/max-stirner/); [acad-promises](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/promises/)

Type: affinity. Evidence items: NEI112@1, NEI113@1, NEI114@1.
Do not infer: exit entails no compensation; withdrawal entails endorsement of fraud.

- NEI112: Joining a voluntary association should not permanently surrender my ability to withdraw from it.
- NEI113: A past commitment alone cannot require me to keep serving a cause indefinitely after I reject its goals.
- NEI114: A voluntary lifelong pledge can remove my moral permission to leave even if I later wholly reject its purpose.

## NE20: Reliance-based reasons for performance or repair
Reasons generated by induced expectations and reliance; separable from promises as normative powers and from specific-performance enforcement. [acad-promises](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/promises/)

Type: monopolar. Evidence items: NEI115@1, NEI116@1, NEI117@1.
Do not infer: rejecting promise-based duties rejects reliance duties; repair requires compelled personal service.

- NEI115: If I intentionally lead someone to incur costs by relying on me, that reliance can give me a reason to make them whole.
- NEI116: Even without a formal promise, deliberately encouraging costly reliance can create responsibilities toward the person who relied.
- NEI117: If I abandon a commitment, the costs I knowingly induced someone else to incur never give me a reason to repair their loss.

## NE21: Elective other-regarding concern
Possibility of voluntarily caring about another as an end without making universal self-sacrifice a duty. Not the intensity of experienced affection. [acad-stirner](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/max-stirner/); [acad-egoism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/egoism/)

Type: affinity. Evidence items: NEI118@1, NEI119@1, NEI120@1.
Do not infer: rejection of obligatory altruism entails no affection; voluntary caring proves hidden self-interest.

- NEI118: I can choose to care about another person for their own sake without treating that concern as a duty owed to everyone.
- NEI119: Freely embracing a relationship can make another person’s good matter to me even without a moral command to sacrifice.
- NEI120: Unless helping someone is a moral obligation, their welfare cannot be a genuine end of my action.

## EP15: Fallibilism about knowledge
Whether knowledge can coexist with the possibility of error; distinct from willingness to revise, skepticism, and infallibility of a nonpropositional perceptual process. [acad-epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemology/); [acad-pragmatism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pragmatism/); [acad-rand-epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ayn-rand/supplement.html)

Type: affinity. Evidence items: EPI100@1, EPI101@1, EPI102@1.
Do not infer: fallible judgments disqualify Objectivism; fallibilism denies knowledge.

- EPI100: Someone can know a claim even though their justification does not eliminate every possibility of error.
- EPI101: The possibility that new evidence might overturn a conclusion does not automatically prevent it from counting as knowledge now.
- EPI102: A claim counts as knowledge only if the possibility of being mistaken has been completely eliminated.

## EP16: Pragmatic clarification through consequences
Clarifying disputed ideas through conceivable differences in experience or conduct; not treating expediency as truth. [acad-pragmatism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pragmatism/)

Type: affinity. Evidence items: EPI103@1, EPI104@1, EPI105@1.
Do not infer: pragmatism entails relativism; pragmatism entails atheism.

- EPI103: To clarify a disputed idea, we should ask what difference accepting it would make to possible experience or action.
- EPI104: When two formulations have no conceivable difference in their consequences, that is a reason to question whether their disagreement is substantive.
- EPI105: The meaning of a philosophical distinction can always be settled without considering any possible consequence for experience or conduct.

## EP17: Concepts as revisable instruments of inquiry
Conceptual revision and usefulness in inquiry; compatible with constraints from mind-independent reality. [acad-pragmatism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pragmatism/); [acad-rand-epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ayn-rand/supplement.html); [acad-marschall](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-019-02463-8)

Type: affinity. Evidence items: EPI106@1, EPI107@1, EPI108@1.
Do not infer: human-made concepts entail arbitrary truth; revising definitions excludes Rand.

- EPI106: Concepts are instruments of inquiry whose usefulness can improve when we revise them.
- EPI107: A classification can be useful and answerable to reality even though people developed it for particular purposes.
- EPI108: Once a concept is established, changing its boundaries necessarily makes our understanding less objective.

## EP18: Noninferential foundations of justification
Whether some epistemic warrant is not derived from other beliefs. Foundationalism need not make every foundation infallible. [acad-epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemology/); [acad-rand-epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ayn-rand/supplement.html)

Type: affinity. Evidence items: EPI109@1, EPI110@1, EPI111@1.
Do not infer: foundationalism entails infallibility; anti-foundationalism equals pragmatism.

- EPI109: Some beliefs can have justification that does not come from inference from other beliefs.
- EPI110: An ordinary perceptual experience can provide initial warrant without first being supported by a further argument.
- EPI111: Every justified belief must receive all of its justification from other beliefs.

## EP19: Contextual certainty
Whether sufficient evidence in a context can warrant certainty despite possible later revision. Not dogmatic immunity to evidence. [acad-rand-epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ayn-rand/supplement.html); [acad-epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemology/)

Type: affinity. Evidence items: EPI112@1, EPI113@1, EPI114@1.
Do not infer: certainty necessarily means timeless immunity to correction.

- EPI112: Calling a conclusion certain can mean that all relevant evidence now available supports it, rather than that revision is forever impossible.
- EPI113: A later discovery need not show that earlier confidence was unreasonable given the evidence available at the time.
- EPI114: A conclusion cannot ever have been warranted as certain if later evidence requires revising it.

## EP20: Perceptual-conceptual basis of factual warrant
Grounding claims about the concrete external world in sensory evidence integrated by reasoning; not a priori rationalism or rejecting scientific inference. [acad-rand-epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ayn-rand/supplement.html); [acad-epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemology/)

Type: affinity. Evidence items: EPI115@1, EPI116@1, EPI117@1.
Do not infer: reason alone means a priori deduction; indirect inference lacks sensory grounding.

- EPI115: Knowledge about concrete reality ultimately requires evidence from experience organized and assessed through reasoning.
- EPI116: Even highly abstract factual claims need a defensible connection to what can be observed.
- EPI117: Pure reasoning can establish new facts about concrete external reality without any ultimate dependence on experience.

## OM11: Abstract-object commitment
Existence of abstract objects such as numbers, distinct from realism about universals and ordinary physical objects. [acad-nominalism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nominalism-metaphysics/); [acad-marschall](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-019-02463-8)

Type: categorical. Evidence items: OMI100@1, OMI101@1, OMI102@1.
Do not infer: abstract objects equal universals; abstract-object denial entails denial of tables.

- OMI100: At least some numbers exist as abstract objects rather than merely as written marks or mental images.
- OMI101: There can be objects that are neither located in space nor able to cause physical events.
- OMI102: Only concrete things exist; apparent reference to abstract objects need not commit us to their existence.

## OM12: Universals commitment
Whether numerically the same universal is instantiated by distinct things; separate from shared resemblance and mental concepts. [acad-nominalism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nominalism-metaphysics/); [acad-rand-epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ayn-rand/supplement.html)

Type: categorical. Evidence items: OMI103@1, OMI104@1, OMI105@1.
Do not infer: rejecting universals denies objective resemblance; concept theory settles all abstract ontology.

- OMI103: Two distinct red objects can instantiate one and the same universal redness.
- OMI104: What explains shared properties is sometimes a single repeatable entity present in many different things.
- OMI105: Distinct things can resemble one another without sharing a numerically identical universal.

## OM13: Ordinary external-world independence
Independence of ordinary concrete objects from thought; does not settle social ontology, universals, or the method of ontology. [acad-rand-epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ayn-rand/supplement.html); [acad-epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemology/)

Type: monopolar. Evidence items: OMI106@1, OMI107@1, OMI108@1.
Do not infer: world realism entails abstract realism; social construction entails idealism.

- OMI106: Mountains and stones can exist independently of anyone thinking about them.
- OMI107: Changing everybody’s beliefs about an unobserved physical object would not by itself change that object’s physical properties.
- OMI108: Ordinary physical objects depend for their existence on being thought about or experienced by a mind.

## OM14: Easy or deflationary approach to ontology
Using conceptual application conditions and ordinary empirical inquiry rather than an additional heavyweight existence test. Compatibility with realism remains disputed. [acad-marschall](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-019-02463-8); [acad-thomasson](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/ontology-made-easy-9780190878665)

Type: affinity. Evidence items: OMI109@1, OMI110@1, OMI111@1.
Do not infer: easy ontology entails nominalism; deflationary method denies existence.

- OMI109: Some existence questions can be answered using a concept’s ordinary application rules together with relevant empirical facts.
- OMI110: After ordinary criteria for saying something exists are satisfied, a further philosophical test of whether it really exists may add nothing.
- OMI111: Even when ordinary conceptual and empirical criteria are met, a separate deep metaphysical test is always required to establish existence.

## SO14: Institutional dependence without elimination
Whether institution-dependent entities can be real; different from independence from all persons or a moral collective duty. [acad-social-ontology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/social-ontology/)

Type: affinity. Evidence items: SOI100@1, SOI101@1, SOI102@1.
Do not infer: social dependence entails fiction; social entities entail moral collectivism.

- SOI100: Money can be real even though its monetary status depends on human practices.
- SOI101: An institution can have genuine effects without existing independently of all the people and practices that sustain it.
- SOI102: If an institution depends on human practices for its existence, it cannot be a real part of the world.

## SO15: Individual-level explanation of social processes
Whether social explanations must be grounded in agents and their interactions; not normative individualism or denial of aggregate patterns. [acad-social-ontology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/social-ontology/)

Type: affinity. Evidence items: SOI103@1, SOI104@1, SOI105@1.
Do not infer: methodological individualism entails selfishness; group agency entails collectivist morality.

- SOI103: An explanation of an institution should show how people’s actions and interactions sustain it.
- SOI104: Saying that a society wanted something leaves an explanation incomplete unless we identify the relevant agents and processes.
- SOI105: Social institutions can be fully explained without any account of the people or interactions through which they operate.

## PL27: Preinstitutional grounding of rights
Whether persons have basic rights independently of social recognition; distinct from the form and strength of a particular property regime. [acad-rights](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/rights/); [acad-rand](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ayn-rand/)

Type: affinity. Evidence items: PLI100@1, PLI101@1, PLI102@1.
Do not infer: strong rights entail one unique moral foundation; natural rights entail a state.

- PLI100: People can possess basic moral rights before any government or community recognizes those rights.
- PLI101: An institution can violate a person’s rights even when every applicable legal rule authorizes its action.
- PLI102: All rights derive entirely from established social rules rather than any claim persons have before those rules.

## PL28: Institutional and instrumental justification of rights
Justification of rights in terms of agency, cooperation or institutional consequences. May coexist with status-based justification. [acad-rights](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/rights/); [acad-contractarianism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/contractarianism/)

Type: affinity. Evidence items: PLI103@1, PLI104@1, PLI105@1.
Do not infer: instrumental justification entails weak rights; instrumental rights entail utilitarianism.

- PLI103: Part of the justification for protecting rights can be what stable rights make possible for human agency and cooperation.
- PLI104: Evaluating alternative rights arrangements can legitimately involve comparing how the resulting institutions work.
- PLI105: How a rights arrangement affects people’s lives and cooperation is never relevant to its justification.

## PL29: Territorial monopoly of final legal force
Whether a single final rights-protecting legal authority is necessary within a territory. Separate from arbitrary command and unqualified obedience. [acad-rand](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ayn-rand/); [acad-legitimacy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/legitimacy/); [acad-hasnas](https://doi.org/10.1093/9780197784631.003.0009)

Type: affinity. Evidence items: PLI106@1, PLI107@1, PLI108@1.
Do not infer: rights protection entails general duty to obey all laws; philosophical anarchism entails rejecting courts.

- PLI106: Protecting rights requires one institution with final legal authority over the use of force within a territory.
- PLI107: Competing agencies must ultimately be subject to a single public legal authority rather than each claiming final enforcement power.
- PLI108: There need not be a single territorial institution with final authority over all legitimate protective force.

## PL30: Nonstate adjudication and protective provision
Institutional possibility and acceptability of nonstate legal services; separate from rejection of general political obligation and from assuming all commons require markets. [acad-hasnas](https://doi.org/10.1093/9780197784631.003.0009); [acad-legitimacy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/legitimacy/)

Type: affinity. Evidence items: PLI109@1, PLI110@1, PLI111@1.
Do not infer: polycentricity entails total state abolition; nonstate provision proves all practical cases succeed.

- PLI109: Courts and protective services could in principle be provided by competing nonstate institutions under shared rules.
- PLI110: Providing adjudication or protection need not give one organization an exclusive territorial right to provide that service.
- PLI111: Even well-coordinated nonstate institutions cannot legitimately provide final adjudication and protective enforcement.

## PL31: Institutional value independent of obedience
Practical justification and participation without conceding a general content-independent moral duty. Not a disguised abolition scale. [acad-obligation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/political-obligation/); [acad-legitimacy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/legitimacy/); [acad-simmons](https://doi.org/10.1086/233944)

Type: affinity. Evidence items: PLI112@1, PLI113@1, PLI114@1.
Do not infer: no general obedience implies no useful public institutions.

- PLI112: A government can provide valuable services without that fact creating a general moral duty to obey its laws.
- PLI113: Using courts or voting can be sensible even for someone who denies that legal commands create moral duties merely by being law.
- PLI114: Anyone who denies a general duty to obey the law must also regard every government institution as useless.

## PL32: Institutional state-abolition preference
Preference for replacing states, independent of rejecting general obligation. This does not measure timing or methods of transition. [acad-obligation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/political-obligation/); [acad-legitimacy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/legitimacy/); [acad-hasnas](https://doi.org/10.1093/9780197784631.003.0009)

Type: affinity. Evidence items: PLI115@1, PLI116@1, PLI117@1.
Do not infer: philosophical anarchism entails immediate abolition; abolition entails violent methods.

- PLI115: Even a relatively decent state should ultimately be replaced by institutions without state authority.
- PLI116: My preferred institutional endpoint has no organization possessing the authority of a state.
- PLI117: A suitably constrained state may be worth retaining even if no general duty to obey it can be established.

## PL33: Contractual remedies distinct from continuing assent
Justification of compensatory or protective remedies despite withdrawal, distinct from compulsory personal performance or legal advice about existing law. [acad-promises](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/promises/)

Type: affinity. Evidence items: PLI118@1, PLI119@1, PLI120@1.
Do not infer: withdrawal permission rules out remedies; enforceability proves moral promise theories.

- PLI118: Someone may be free to stop performing a personal commitment while still owing compensation for losses they knowingly induced.
- PLI119: A rule requiring repair after breaking an agreement need not require forcing the person to perform the promised personal service.
- PLI120: Once a person withdraws assent from an agreement, any claim for compensation necessarily becomes illegitimate.

## AH13: Psychological egoism
Descriptive universal claim that ultimate motives concern one’s own welfare. Distinguish desire ownership from desire content. [acad-egoism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/egoism/); [acad-stirner](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/max-stirner/)

Type: monopolar. Evidence items: AHI100@1, AHI101@1, AHI102@1.
Do not infer: acting on my desires means desiring only my own welfare.

- AHI100: Every intentional action is ultimately motivated by the actor’s own welfare, even when the action appears generous.
- AHI101: When someone helps another for no reward, their ultimate aim is still always some benefit to themselves.
- AHI102: Someone can ultimately desire another person’s good rather than only the benefits that helping brings to themselves.

## AH14: Incompatibilist condition on free agency
Whether causal determinism excludes free agency, distinct from the belief that determinism is true or ordinary freedom from coercion. [acad-fwi](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24561311/); [acad-rand-epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ayn-rand/supplement.html)

Type: categorical. Evidence items: AHI103@1, AHI104@1, AHI105@1.
Do not infer: free will and determinism are opposite sliders; uncaused randomness alone establishes agency.

- AHI103: If the entire prior state of the world fixed a choice, that choice could not be genuinely free.
- AHI104: Genuine freedom requires that the person could choose differently with exactly the same complete prior conditions.
- AHI105: A choice can be genuinely free even if it was determined, provided it issued appropriately from the person’s own reasoning and capacities.
