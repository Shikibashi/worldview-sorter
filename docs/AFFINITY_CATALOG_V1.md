# Philosophical affinity catalog 1.0.0

The current [affinity expansion review](AFFINITY_EXPANSION_REVIEW.md) screens additional scoped positions and traditions against the active model. It does not alter this historical catalog or activate new public comparisons.

**Current release:** [Catalog 1.2.0](../data/affinities/catalog-v1.2.json) adds one scoped Easy Ontology comparison to the six historical entries. [Model release 1.4.0](../data/releases/model-release-v1.4.0.json) pins the exact catalog and interpretation model. Easy Ontology's method is assessed only on Full; its broader application and realism dispute remain visible gaps. The six-entry [catalog 1.1.0](../data/affinities/catalog-v1.1.json) and original catalog 1.0.0 remain pinned for historical results. The remainder of this document describes the original six-entry catalog unless stated otherwise.

The first [catalog](../data/affinities/catalog-v1.json) compares a respondent's **already interpreted public propositions** with selected source-backed doctrines. Affinity is overlap or divergence with doctrine, never membership in a tradition. A person can overlap with several entries at once. A missing defining commitment stays unmeasured; it is never filled by a neighboring belief, raw answer, political coordinate, or market preference. The catalog is theoretical editorial content for the frozen pilot, not a validated classifier.

## Architecture and semantics

This catalog's instrument is `worldview-pilot-1.0.0` (`philosophy-pilot-1.0.0`, 238 exact item revisions), interpreted by `generic-1.0.0-pilot` with `pilot-result-states-1.0.0`. The catalog is `philosophical-affinity-1.0.0` under `doctrinal-comparison-1.0.0`. Its [manifest](../data/affinities/manifest-v1.json) pins the catalog bytes. The catalog validator accepts later semantic-versioned catalog definitions only under comparison semantics it knows how to evaluate; release manifests still pin the actual catalog bytes. The [schema](../schemas/philosophical-affinity-catalog.schema.json) distinguishes doctrine roles, mapping status, sources, scope, non-entailments, neighbors, and disputed commitments. The evaluator receives only the interpretation report's public direct propositions and declared derived propositions. Runtime and affinity validators require public and research-only rule sets to be disjoint. The release gate requires an exact, rule-linked, source-supported public proposition for any new or changed direct or partial doctrinal mapping. A derived criterion also requires an exact, source-linked derived conclusion and exact, source-linked public prerequisites; the release gate checks new derived criteria against the same boundary. The evaluator requires one row for every public proposition and defined derived result, rejecting duplicate or missing interpreted rows before comparison. Research-only rows may accompany the report but cannot satisfy doctrine. No derived proposition is needed by this first catalog.

For each criterion, **overlap** requires a settled proposition in the expected direction; **divergence** requires a settled proposition in the opposite direction. A lean is unresolved, even when it points toward the criterion. Mixed evidence is contradictory and retained, not averaged. `not_measured`, a doctrine without an appropriate pilot proposition, and doctrine unsuitable for quiz inference are unmeasured. A **partial** mapping remains partial even when its linked proposition is supported. Disputed commitments are shown for context and never disqualify a comparison. A defining divergence yields `material_divergence`; if no defining criterion overlaps, the state is `no_sufficiently_established_affinity`; otherwise unmeasured or unresolved defining doctrine remains named. Only when all defining criteria overlap and none is missing does `overlap_on_measured_core` appear. These are logical editorial descriptions, not empirical cutoffs or match scores.

The UI places independent tradition cards under the proposition result. It shows the comparison state and short reasons, with doctrine, gaps, neighbors, non-entailments, and sources inside inspectable details. Each mapped criterion names its mapping status, the relevant interpreted rule text, its evidence state, and links to the sources declared for that criterion; unmapped or unsuitable doctrine remains explicitly without a pilot proposition. A mapped criterion whose rule has an inherited scope or lacks a rule-linked supporting claim names that limitation in its own detail; its overlap label says **authored overlap under review**. Only 15 public pilot rules store an explicit standalone `proposition`; for the other 126 the result names the inherited authored `scope` as such. A shared explanation carries that distinction too. This is a metadata provenance boundary, not a claim that all 15 explicit propositions are scientifically validated. Validation requires every criterion source to belong to its tradition's declared source set. The topic result exposes underlying answers and source explanation. Entries without defining overlap remain under “Other traditions inspected.” There is no single winner, ordering by score, probability, percentage, or identity claim. An exported result records catalog, model, instrument, and interpretation-semantics versions. A completed local quiz backup also pins its catalog version; resume loads that release from the version registry and fails closed if it is missing.

The governance dependency report identifies 16 of this catalog's 18 direct or partial criterion mappings as referring to rules with only a legacy `scope` label. All 18 mappings lack a rule-linked supporting source claim, including the two that have an explicit proposition. These gaps limit claim-level traceability of the doctrinal comparison, including defining criteria. The current version remains reproducible; a future catalog/model release must review those exact mappings rather than treating a scope string or a topic citation as full support. These counts describe editorial metadata gaps, not an empirical estimate of affinity accuracy.

The [pragmatism mapping review](governance/PRAGMATISM_MAPPING_REVIEW.md) identifies a specific route-level gap: the frozen route omits `EPI103@1`, so a supported `EP16` result can rest on `EPI104@1` plus disagreement with the absolute reverse wording of `EPI105@1`. That second answer does not uniquely establish the pragmatic maxim. The historical rule and comparison remain reproducible, while the respondent-facing affinity presentation marks the inherited-scope criterion as under review.

The compact result flags a defining overlap backed by inherited scope text, and current `worldview-share-6` affinity cards keep mapped propositions separate from inherited rule scopes while retaining the linked interpretation state. Version-6 selected result rows additionally preserve source-claim roles; version-5 cards remain readable with their older citation detail. A partial doctrinal mapping is labeled unresolved even if the linked interpretation is supported or opposed. That disclosure does not change the frozen criterion mapping or the respondent's historical evidence state.

The presentation layer now withholds a close-affinity label when any defining mapped criterion relies on a public rule that has only a legacy scope and no separately recorded proposition. This affects all six initial traditions. The frozen authored comparison output and catalog stay available for inspection and replay; `affinityPresentation` records the respondent-facing qualification separately. A future catalog release can lift the qualification only after its defining mappings point to reviewed exact propositions with adequate answer and source support.

## Accepted entries and pilot reach

Counts are doctrinal criteria in the authored catalog, not factor loadings or respondent scores. “Direct core” means the pilot has a public interpreted proposition that can bear on a defining commitment; actual respondents may still have insufficient or mixed evidence.

**Pragmatism.** The [SEP account](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pragmatism/) identifies Peirce's pragmatic maxim as central and fallibilist inquiry as common, while documenting disagreements over realism and truth. [Peirce's 1878 formulation](https://www.peirce.org/writings/p119.html) is the primary anchor. `construct-EP16` tests conceptual clarification by conceivable consequences; `construct-EP15` is characteristic but cannot stand in for it. Complex, revisable knowledge is only a partial method marker. Pragmatism is not coded as “whatever works is true.”

**Stirnerian ownness and ethical egoism.** [Leopold's Stirner entry](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/max-stirner/) explains why ownness is not psychological egoism, a fixed self-interest duty, nihilism, or a specific economic program. [Stirner's text](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/34580) is included with a note about the older translation. Ownness, continued endorsement of association, and rejection of a promise's independent defeasible moral force are separate defining comparisons (`construct-NE17`, `construct-NE19`, `construct-NE18`). [Shaver's egoism entry](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/egoism/) distinguishes the normative moral thesis from rational and psychological egoism. Only `construct-NE15` directly tests the weaker universal moral-priority prescription; the stronger if-and-only-if account of rightness is unmeasured. The two entries can diverge while sharing personal freedom or self-regard.

**Philosophical anarchism.** [SEP on anarchism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/anarchism/) and [political obligation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/political-obligation/) distinguish skepticism about content-independent state authority from activism, abolition, and institutional design. The no-general-duty proposition is direct. `construct-PL31` offers only partial evidence about the broader legitimacy thesis; it also bears on the state-tolerant strand as a characteristic commitment. `audit2-PL05-decentralization` and `ph-polycentric-order` never satisfy either defining criterion.

**Objectivism.** [The SEP Rand account](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ayn-rand/) and [Rand's ethics essay](https://courses.aynrand.org/works/the-objectivist-ethics/) ground this system in realism, perception and reason, life-based objective ethics, rational self-interest, rights, and a rights-protecting state. The pilot directly compares the ethics and epistemology commitments. Ordinary mind-independent objects give only partial evidence for Rand's broader metaphysical realism. Its basic-rights and legal-authority rules are not measured by the fixed route. Market-coordination answers are partial characteristic context, never a route around those missing political commitments. Rand's intrinsic/subjective/objective distinction has scholarly interpretive disagreement and is not used as a hard gate.

**Ontological naturalism.** [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/naturalism/) distinguishes ontological from methodological naturalism and cautions that the label is imprecise; [physicalism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/physicalism/) is a separate metaphysical thesis. Opposition to explicit supernatural reality bears directly on one defining criterion. The pilot's broader `natural-world` proposition is not measured, so even that opposition cannot close the core. Scientific testability is partial characteristic context, not ontology. Atheism alone is ignored for this mapping.

## Considered and deferred

The first research screen also considered these adjacent candidates. Deferral is a content judgment about this pilot, not a claim that the tradition is philosophically unimportant.

| Candidate | Reason for deferral |
| --- | --- |
| Market anarchism | Nonstate legal-service acceptability and market coordination do not establish property, contract, and market-based institutional doctrine. [Anarchism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/anarchism/) distinguishes market and social anarchisms. |
| Classical or radical liberalism | Liberty, rights, property, and state scope vary across liberal traditions; the pilot's rights and strong-property rules are not measured. [Libertarianism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/libertarianism/) documents the property and self-ownership dispute. |
| Utilitarian and broader consequentialist families | `outcomes-count` says consequences matter, not that rightness depends only on outcomes or that welfare is the sole value. [Consequentialism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/consequentialism/) and [rule consequentialism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/consequentialism-rule/) distinguish these. |
| Scanlonian contractualism | `reasonable-rejection` is only partial evidence; the pilot lacks the individual-versus-aggregate and wrongness-grounding discriminators. [Contractualism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/contractualism/) states both distinctions. |
| Minimal moral realism, error theory, expressivism, and constructivist metaethics | Stance independence is stronger and disputed within realism; truth-aptness is not measured, and the pilot cannot distinguish error theory from noncognitivism or the constructivist family. [Moral Realism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-realism/) gives the relevant taxonomy. |
| Physicalism | The broad `physical-reality` rule is not measured. A view about a complete physical explanation of experience does not settle the universal metaphysical thesis. [Physicalism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/physicalism/) states that broader claim. |

Kantian ethics, virtue ethics, dualism, idealism, existentialist families, Stoicism, and secular humanism were outside this deliberately narrow first research screen; no catalog decision about their mature doctrine has been made. The pilot currently has neighboring propositions, but those do not justify adding them by analogy. No new questions or revisions were made for affinity coverage.

## Validation boundary

The catalog regression suite checks positive and negative interpreted states, partial and missing evidence, leans, contradictions, unmeasured core, false-positive neighbors, simultaneous affinities, no close affinity, research/deprecated isolation, raw-answer rejection, summary integration, and version/hash replay. Browser validation checks that the comparison is rendered without percentages. These synthetic fixtures verify software and authored logical boundaries, not participant comprehension, reliability, local independence, or philosophical self-identification. The prior 49-construct audit and 240-item content review retain their coverage gaps; this catalog does not promote them.

Human response data are needed for cognitive comprehension of doctrinal prompts, item wording and reverse-key effects, local dependence, subgroup interpretation, and whether the pilot's interpreted propositions predict independently elicited philosophical judgments. Those are empirical questions for a later pilot, not issues resolved by adding more catalog logic.
