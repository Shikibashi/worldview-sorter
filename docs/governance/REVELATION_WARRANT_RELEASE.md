# EP10 first-person revelation warrant: successor decision

**Status:** approved for model release 1.13.0; final validation pending. Historical `model-release-1.12.0` and earlier artifacts remain unchanged.

## Exact claim and evidence

> An apparent divine revelation can sometimes give its experiencer an initial factual reason in favor of its content before independent verification, even if that reason may later be defeated.

This is a respondent's view about a *possible* first-person epistemic reason. It is not a finding that any actual revelation occurred or that sincerity makes a claim true. The active predecessor says “some factual justification” but counts disagreement with `EPI021@1`, which never identifies whose justification is at issue, while excluding `EPI032@1`'s explicit `some` response.

| Revision | New use | Support | Oppose | Nondirectional |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `EPI032@1` | Amount of factual justification for the experiencer in a clear seeming revelation case | `substantial`, `some` | `none` | `little`, no view, not understood, missing |
| `EPI122@1` | Distinguish a first-person factual reason from a reason merely to investigate | `initial_reason` | `investigation_only` | `depends_on_case`, no view, not understood, missing |
| `EPI021@1` | Retained only for historical/research access | None for the successor | None for the successor | Every answer for this rule |

Two authored directional units are required for supported or opposed. One unit can yield only a direction-qualified lean. Opposite directions produce mixed/context-dependent evidence. An omitted route remains not measured; presented but unanswered or nondirectional evidence is insufficient. The two current items use related revelation framing, so authored unit IDs do not establish empirical independence or local-dependence absence.

The new item is necessary because `EPI032@1` distinguishes *amount* but can leave the respondent's “some” answer vulnerable to an interpretation as merely a reason to investigate. `EPI122@1` asks whether the experience counts in favor of its content at all, while allowing a context-dependent answer. It does not ask the outsider to take the experiencer's word for it. The unreleased `EPI120@1` draft was rejected for this release because its options bundle experiencer strength with outsider warrant and substantially repeat `EPI032@1`. The separate foundationalism draft `EPI121@1` remains reserved.

## Source scope

[SEP Religious Experience §3](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/religious-experience/) describes defeasible arguments for the epistemic value of religious experience, rival defeaters, and the distinct warrant of the subject and a hearer. [SEP Divine Revelation §2](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/divine-revelation/) describes inferential, noninferential, and hybrid approaches. These sources support the philosophical *contrast* and the possibility that a respondent could endorse it. They do not show that the experience is veridical, determine how much warrant any actual case has, validate the new item, or calibrate the two-unit threshold. The rule-linked claim is recorded separately in the versioned source ledger.

## Neighbors and non-entailments

- A reason to investigate a claim is distinct from an initial factual reason **in favor** of it.
- Modest, defeasible first-person warrant does not establish substantial standalone justification or enough reason for settled belief.
- An outsider may lack the experiencer's grounds; neither outsider testimony nor public institutional warrant follows.
- The answer does not establish religious identity, the truth of any divine message, intervention, moral authority, or political authority.
- `little` may express negligible but nonzero justification; it is too imprecise to use as opposition to the new possibility claim.
