# Total-well-being maximization: scoped act-rightness review

**Disposition:** promote one narrow direct proposition in the successor Full route only. Do not infer utilitarian identity or add an affinity criterion.

## Exact proposition

> In the general rule item and the specified promise case, the respondent treats choosing the available action with the greatest total well-being for everyone affected as decisive for individual-act moral rightness, even when keeping a valid promise favors the lower-well-being action.

This is a directly interpreted response pattern: one general statement plus one explicit application. It is not a latent construct score or a claim that the respondent consistently acts on the principle.

## Why the active bank does not already establish it

The active model separately asks whether consequences can count as moral reasons, whether individual acts or consequence-justified rules determine rightness, and whether well-being exhausts ultimate outcome value. None directly asks whether total well-being must be maximized as the criterion of rightness when a promise points the other way. NEI125@1 is an unreleased draft and its “no other effects differ” wording leaves the effects of promise-breaking unclear. It remains preserved; NEI125@2 makes the welfare comparison explicit and includes trust effects.

NEI133@1 asks for the general criterion in plain language. NEI125@2 checks whether the respondent applies that criterion in a promise conflict. They are complementary authored evidence units, not empirically independent indicators. Their local dependence and comprehension remain untested.

## Evidence and source basis

- [SEP, “Consequentialism,” §§1–3](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/consequentialism/): distinguishes maximizing act-rightness, the account of outcome value, and total versus average aggregation. The relevant discussion treats classic utilitarianism as a conjunction of separable theses.
- [SEP, “Promises,” §6.3](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/promises/): contrasts act-utilitarian explanations of promise obligations through effects with rule-utilitarian evaluation of practices. Promise conflict is a useful discriminator, not a complete theory label.

These academic sources support the philosophical distinctions. They do not validate the item wording, response process, two-unit threshold, or interpretation behavior.

## Scope, neighbors, and false-positive protections

The distinction is between total-well-being maximization as an individual-act rightness criterion and nearby positions: generic concern for consequences; act-versus-rule criteria; rule consequentialism; promise-based duties; taking welfare as one important reason without making it decisive; pluralist or distribution-sensitive outcome value; and total-versus-average welfare approaches.

Neither NE24 (welfare-only outcome value) nor NE22 (a direct act-consequence criterion) is used as a substitute for these items. An answer about NE22, NE23, promises, impartial concern, rights, or welfare value alone cannot support this proposition. A response pattern does not establish utilitarianism, hedonism, a theory of personal well-being, an all-things-considered distribution rule, population ethics, a general view about promises, a real-world decision procedure, political policy, or actual behavior.

Likely false positives include respondents who favor a high-welfare outcome in this case for a different reason; treat a valid promise as a welfare-affecting institution but disagree about how trust was stipulated; or endorse the general principle while reserving exceptions outside the item. The output therefore names only the tested criterion and case. The vignette states that the welfare comparison includes effects on trust and that no other consequences differ; if a respondent still selects a promise-based answer, that is opposing evidence to this scoped criterion, not a consistency error.

## Route and affinity consequences

The successor Full route includes both exact revisions, spaced apart in its existing order. Quick and Standard retain their existing item references and report this proposition as `not_measured`; optional clarification can only use these items through the existing evidence planner and cannot convert omitted route content into opposition. The rule is not mapped into any of the 11 philosophical comparisons. In particular, it does not add a utilitarian or act-consequentialist affinity criterion.

## Historical compatibility and limits

The previous bank, model, Full route, progressive policy, catalog, engine snapshot, and model release remain unchanged. NEI125@1 stays in the unreleased draft; it is not edited in place. Historical administrations continue to resolve through their original manifests. The new release is a current interpretation, not a rewrite of earlier results.

This is authored philosophical content, not an empirically validated scale. Real response data and cognitive interviews would be needed to assess comprehension, response behavior, route uptake, and whether the two items add distinguishable information.
