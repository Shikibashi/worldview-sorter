# Welfarist outcome value, proposed release 1.17.0

This is an authored distinction, not a validated respondent scale. The historical `NEI124@1` [draft](../../data/items/affinity-gap-draft-v1.json) asks about what improves an outcome, but its `rights_priority` alternative can coexist with welfare as the only source of *outcome* value: a right can constrain an action without making its result better. The draft is preserved; the production successor is `NEI124@2` with a clearer contrast.

## Proposition and sources

The proposed direct proposition is: **Only how well affected beings fare ultimately makes an outcome better or worse in itself; features such as beauty do not add independent outcome value when no being's well-being changes.** It concerns a theory of outcome value, not a criterion for right action, a required sum of welfare, hedonism, or utilitarian identity.

[SEP Well-Being, section 5.1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/well-being/) describes welfarism as the view that well-being is the only ultimate value, while observing disputes about its interpretation and the relationship to rights and equality. [SEP Consequentialism, section 2](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/consequentialism/) distinguishes a welfarist theory of value from plural theories that include beauty, truth, justice, or other goods. [SEP Environmental Ethics, section 2](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-environmental/) explains the disputed idea that nonhuman nature can have non-instrumental value independently of its usefulness to people. These sources define the distinctions; they do not establish that respondents understand or answer the original questions as intended.

## Minimum direct evidence

| Item | Directional meaning | Distinct contribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `NEI124@2` production successor | Welfare-only outcome value supports; an independent nonwelfare good opposes; a contextual or other answer is nondirectional. | States the general value claim while explicitly setting aside whether an action is permissible. |
| `NEI132@1` | Denying independent value to an unseen, lifeless beautiful landscape supports; granting it intrinsic outcome value opposes; uncertainty is nondirectional. | Tests one concrete nonwelfare counterexample without using policy, religion, or ideology labels. |

Two concordant authored units are required for a supported or opposed result. Discordance is mixed; one directional unit leans; unanswered, no-view, not-understood, and nondirectional answers do not become support. The two questions are conceptually related and may be locally dependent in human responses; no empirical independence is claimed. A person may reject the landscape's value while holding that truth or justice independently improves an outcome, so the general item remains necessary.

The current `VA20` registry object concerns *what constitutes a person's well-being*. That is a different question from whether well-being is the only ultimate *outcome value*. Neither `NEI004@2` (equal weight for distant welfare) nor `NE22` (which acts are right) supplies the missing value thesis. A narrow new `NE24` authored construct keeps these layers separate.

## False-positive boundaries

- A person can accept welfare-only outcome value while holding that a promise or right independently constrains an action. No act-consequentialist or utilitarian result follows.
- Welfarism need not identify welfare with pleasure, desire satisfaction, or any one existing `VA20` account.
- Welfarism need not require maximizing a simple total: distribution-sensitive welfarist views remain possible.
- A refusal to attribute beauty to an unseen landscape is insufficient alone; a person may still assign independent value to justice, truth, or something else.
- Affirming beauty's independent value rejects the scoped welfare-only thesis, but does not identify an environmental philosophy or any complete ethical theory.

The Full route may gain the two exact items as an authored 245-question successor. Quick and Standard should remain unchanged and report this proposition as not measured. This adds one content distinction; it does not create a new philosophical-affinity entry. Historical routes, item wording, and interpretations remain pinned.
