# Sacred-status weight in one tradeoff, release 1.16.0

This successor resolves the overbroad result wording identified in the [predecessor review](SACRED_VALUE_REVIEW.md). It is an authored interpretation, not psychometric validation. Model release 1.15.0 and all earlier item, route, rule, source, catalog, and engine artifacts remain available for historical replay.

## Exact public proposition

The ph-sacred-value rule now reports: “In the stated community-object and practical-benefit case, sacred status itself can carry at least some moral weight beyond the object's ordinary resource value and the specified direct harm to persons.” The result concerns a respondent's judgment about the described object and choice. It does not identify a general sacred-values disposition.

[Rotondo's peer-reviewed article](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/philosophy/article/abs/philosophical-significance-of-the-sacred/7B1A523474922B7051EAC55F9760B5BA) discusses ethical implications of sacredness. Its publisher abstract supplies conceptual context for asking about sacred-status reasons, while the narrower community-object case comes from the actual items. [Katsafanas's academic chapter](https://academic.oup.com/book/44864/chapter-abstract/384569224) defines a stronger account with inviolability, incontestability, and dialectical invulnerability. [SEP's discussion of incommensurable values](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/value-incommensurable/) distinguishes several kinds of comparison failure. Neither stronger account follows from assigning some weight here. The prior [Ginges et al. study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17460042/) remains context-limited empirical background, not item validation.

## Evidence and limits

| Exact item | Supporting answers | Opposing answers | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RCI012@1 | Agreement that sacred status can give a reason against treating something as an ordinary resource | Disagreement | Does not ask whether status is intrinsic or attitude-independent. |
| RCI029@1 | Very high or some moral weight in the community-object tradeoff | Only human reactions and relationships matter, or no additional sacred-status weight | “Some” allows the practical benefit to outweigh sacred status; “very high” does not establish a universal ban on tradeoffs. |

The two authored evidence units retain their historical response mappings and two-unit threshold. Concordant answers can support or oppose the narrow proposition; a single directional answer leans; conflict is mixed; presented nondirectional, no-view, and missing answers remain insufficient. Quick and Standard do not administer both required items and leave this result not measured. Full still has 243 questions. These units are semantically related; their empirical independence and respondent comprehension are untested.

A community-conferred status and a secular account can yield the same support as an attitude-independent or religious account. The rule therefore does not infer intrinsic sacredness, deity belief, revelation, religious law, purity morality, value incommensurability, strict inviolability, or a stance toward sacred persons, places, or political commitments. The internal comparison adopts the same limited scope; no philosophical-affinity criterion is added.

The [approved proposal](../../data/governance/proposals/MCP-2026-086.json) and [regression](../../scripts/test-sacred-value-scope-release.mjs) cover positive, negative, mixed, missing, tradeoff-permitting, source-linked, and historical cases. Human response data would be needed to test comprehension, the two-item relation, and any psychometric interpretation; this software release makes none of those claims.
