# Cognitive review protocol

Cognitive review is the gate between **candidate item writing** and **pilot psychometrics**.

It answers a different question from factor analysis:

> Do respondents interpret the item in the way the project thinks they do?

A clean factor loading cannot rescue an item that consistently means something different to respondents than it means to the authors.

## Unit of review

Reviews attach to an exact pair:

```text
item ID + item revision
```

If wording changes materially, increment the revision. Reviews of the old wording remain historical evidence but do not count toward the new revision's promotion gate.

## What to collect

The session schema records:

- the respondent's actual answer;
- their paraphrase of the item;
- their reasoning;
- unclear terms;
- alternate plausible interpretations;
- whether the response options were adequate;
- perceived social-desirability pressure;
- what a midpoint meant if one was used;
- issue tags and severity;
- suggested revisions.

Do not collect direct identifiers in the review record.

## Moderated sequence

For each item:

1. Let the respondent answer without explanation.
2. Ask them to paraphrase the item.
3. Ask why they chose the response.
4. Probe any potentially ambiguous term.
5. Ask whether another interpretation seemed possible.
6. Ask whether the available responses represented their view.
7. Ask whether any answer felt socially expected or stigmatized.
8. If they chose Neutral, No view, Not understood, or Not applicable, ask what that response meant.
9. Ask for a clearer wording only after interpretation has been elicited.

Avoid teaching the intended construct before the respondent has explained their own interpretation.

## Operational sampling

The draft workflow target is:

- at least 3 independent reviews of every exact item revision;
- preferably 5;
- about 20 items per review session.

These are project-management targets for qualitative iteration, not claims about statistical sample adequacy.

Rotate item sets across reviewers rather than making each person evaluate the entire research bank.

## Promotion to pilot

An item may move from `candidate` to `pilot` only when:

- its exact revision meets the cognitive-review coverage target;
- no blocking comprehension problem remains;
- reviewer paraphrases substantially converge on the intended idea;
- response options are adequate;
- ideological cueing or obvious social desirability has been addressed;
- any revision triggered by review has itself been reviewed.

Pilot status means only that the item is ready to collect psychometric data.

It does **not** mean the item is valid, reliable, invariant, or suitable for scoring.

## Review aggregation

Do not average qualitative issue severity into a fake numeric quality score.

Summaries should report:

- review count;
- distribution of comprehension ratings;
- recurring issue tags;
- recurring alternate interpretations;
- proportion reporting high social pressure;
- whether reviewers converge on the intended paraphrase;
- unresolved major/blocking findings.

Human editorial judgment remains necessary before promotion.
