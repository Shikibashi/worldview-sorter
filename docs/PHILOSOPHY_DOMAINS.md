# Philosophy-domain upgrade

This release makes domain coverage a property of question selection and evidence, not just twelve headings. The fields overlap: ontology asks about existence and categories within a broader metaphysical inquiry. The organizational split does not assert separate empirical factors. [Metaphysics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/metaphysics/)

## Actual coverage

The 562-item bank is unchanged. 57 new source-reviewed interpretation rules bring the model to 140. 132 active constructs have mappings; 49 remain explicit gaps.

## Topic structure

- **Metaethics: truth and grounds**: What could make a moral claim true?
- **Metaethics: scope and context**: Do the same requirements apply in different cases?
- **Metaethics: reasons and motivation**: Can moral reasons bind or motivate independently of desire?
- **Normative ethics: reasons for action**: What should guide what we do?
- **Normative ethics: obligations and personal projects**: What do we owe others, and what may we keep for ourselves?
- **Ethics in cases: rules, intentions and harm**: Which features of an action change its moral assessment?
- **Moral psychology: concerns**: Which considerations draw your moral attention?
- **Axiology: personal priorities**: What matters in your own life?
- **Axiology: well-being and aesthetic value**: What makes a life go well, or a judgment of beauty apt?
- **Epistemology: evidence and inquiry**: How do you decide what to believe?
- **Epistemology: justification and science**: What warrants a belief or a scientific claim?
- **Ontology: what exists**: What belongs in an account of what exists?
- **Metaontology: how to ask about existence**: Are existence disputes deep, conceptual, or sometimes verbal?
- **Metaphysics: dependence and purpose**: Are higher-level features reducible, and can nature have purposes?
- **Metaphysics: laws and causes**: Do laws govern events, and what makes something a cause?
- **Metaphysics: time and possibility**: What makes past, future and possible things real?
- **Philosophy of mind: consciousness**: How does experience relate to physical processes?
- **Mind and self: identity and content**: What makes a mind or a person continuous?
- **Agency: freedom and determinism**: What would make a choice genuinely free?
- **Human nature: reasons and causes**: What influences human action?
- **Philosophy of religion: reality and purpose**: What, if anything, lies beyond natural reality?
- **Religion: importance and sacred commitments**: What role do spiritual commitments play?
- **Existential philosophy: meaning**: What can make a life meaningful?
- **Existential orientation: possibility**: How do you regard the possibilities life offers?
- **Social philosophy: persons and explanation**: How do individual claims and social explanations relate?
- **Social ontology: institutions and groups**: How do practices constitute social reality?
- **Political philosophy: legitimate authority**: What could justify institutions and coercion?
- **Political philosophy: liberty and rights**: Which freedoms and claims constrain power?
- **Political economy: property and coordination**: How should ownership and coordination be justified?
- **Legal philosophy: validity and justice**: What makes a law valid or a legal response just?
- **Political philosophy: membership and moral scope**: Who is owed political consideration?

## Selection change

Every route contains complete two-evidence-group bundles for each listed facet. One facet may have several alternative bundles; a seed chooses among them without using answers or identities. Formats are represented and remaining slots add balanced bundles. This is an authored coverage guarantee, not an empirically optimized short form. [2020 PhilPapers Survey: Question Design](https://survey2020.philpeople.org/survey/design/questions)

The new local public form uses a distinct instrument version and packet-policy version. Research pilot packet generation is unchanged. Existing backups without a public-form marker use the historical generator; unknown versions fail closed. The collector does not mistake a new public form for a research packet.

## Exact academic rules

### Moral wrongness can be independent of attitudes
Construct ME01; facet meta-status. [Pölzler (2018), How to Measure Moral Realism](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13164-018-0401-8); [Moral Realism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-realism/)
Evidence: MEI002@2, MEI023@1.
This concerns possible stance-independent truth, not certainty, unanimity, political intolerance or a complete realist theory.

### Equal morally relevant cases have equal requirements
Construct ME02; facet meta-scope. [Moral Particularism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-particularism/)
Evidence: MEI024@1, MEI010@1.
Universal applicability does not establish stance-independent grounds. Context can change what is morally relevant.

### Context can change the appropriate moral judgment
Construct ME03; facet meta-scope. [Moral Particularism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-particularism/)
Evidence: MEI003@2, MEI025@1.
Exceptions and context sensitivity do not entail relativism or the absence of moral principles.

### Particular cases need more than general rules
Construct ME04; facet meta-scope. [Moral Particularism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-particularism/)
Evidence: MEI008@1, MEI015@1.
Supports the importance of case judgment; it does not identify strong particularism or reject all general principles.

### Moral reasons need not serve existing desires
Construct ME05; facet meta-reasons. [Moral Motivation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-motivation/)
Evidence: MEI004@2, MEI007@1, MEI026@1.
A reason to act, actual motivation and an overriding obligation are separate. An answer about one does not supply the others.

### Sincere moral judgment necessarily brings some motivation
Construct ME08; facet meta-reasons. [Moral Motivation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-motivation/)
Evidence: MEI020@1, MEI022@1.
Disagreement rejects a necessary link, not every usual link. This does not decide moral realism or psychological egoism.

### Close relationships can create additional duties
Construct NE09; facet ethics-obligations. [Care Ethics](https://iep.utm.edu/care-ethics/); [Allen Habib: Promises](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/promises/)
Evidence: NEI024@1, NEI033@1.
Some partial obligations can coexist with impartial reasons; they do not establish collectivism or favoritism in every role.

### Personal projects can permissibly outweigh modest gains
Construct NE10; facet ethics-obligations. [Deontological Ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-deontological/); [Robert Shaver: Egoism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/egoism/)
Evidence: NEI011@1, NEI034@1.
A permission to favor a project is not a duty to maximize self-interest.

### Very large benefits can require major sacrifice
Construct NE11; facet ethics-obligations. [Consequentialism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/consequentialism/)
Evidence: NEI012@1, NEI035@1.
This reports demanding obligations, not how much a respondent actually donates or whether they accept utilitarianism.

### Causing harm can be worse than failing to prevent it
Construct NE12; facet ethics-action. [Doing vs. Allowing Harm](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/doing-allowing/)
Evidence: NEI013@1, NEI036@1.
An equally-wrong verdict can arise from further duties; it is not automatically counted as rejecting every doing/allowing distinction.

### Justified rules have weight beyond a single outcome
Construct NE13; facet ethics-action. [Consequentialism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/consequentialism/); [Deontological Ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-deontological/)
Evidence: NEI037@1, NEI040@1.
Not enough to distinguish rule consequentialism, deontology and practice-based ethics.

### Intention matters beyond foreseeable consequences
Construct NE14; facet ethics-action. [Doctrine of Double Effect](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/double-effect/); [Doing vs. Allowing Harm](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/doing-allowing/)
Evidence: NEI041@1, NEI043@1.
This does not establish the full doctrine of double effect. Intention and doing/allowing are not interchangeable.

### Higher-level properties are fully reducible in principle
Construct OM04; facet metaphysical-structure. [Metaphysics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/metaphysics/); [Physicalism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/physicalism/)
Evidence: OMI006@1, OMI007@1.
Irreducibility need not mean supernaturalism. The question concerns a general explanatory commitment, not established scientific reduction.

### Some purposes are built into nature
Construct OM05; facet metaphysical-structure. [Metaphysics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/metaphysics/)
Evidence: OMI004@1, OMI014@1.
Purpose in nature is distinct from a personal creator, ethical purpose and a felt meaningful life.

### Natural laws do more than summarize events
Construct OM06; facet laws-causation. [Laws of Nature](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/laws-of-nature/)
Evidence: OMI016@1, OMI018@1.
Dispositionalism remains an alternative, not a midpoint or rejection of every non-Humean account.

### Laws depend on the total pattern of particular events
Construct OM06; facet laws-causation. [Laws of Nature](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/laws-of-nature/)
Evidence: OMI017@1, OMI018@1.
This narrow Humean-law comparison does not imply moral Humeanism, empiricism or deterministic physics.

### Causation requires a productive connection
Construct OM07; facet laws-causation. [The Metaphysics of Causation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/causation-metaphysics/)
Evidence: OMI020@1, OMI021@1.
Other accounts are not automatically denials. Counterfactual dependence can matter in a mixed account.

### Past and future events are as real as present events
Construct OM09; facet time-modality. [Time](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/time/)
Evidence: OMI026@1, OMI027@1.
Opposition does not establish presentism: growing block and other accounts remain. Eternalism does not itself establish fatalism or determinism.

### Other possible worlds are concrete realities
Construct OM10; facet time-modality. [Possible Worlds](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/possible-worlds/)
Evidence: OMI029@1, OMI030@1.
Rejecting concrete worlds does not identify one alternative. Merely considering possibilities does not endorse concrete-world realism.

### Strong contrary evidence should reopen beliefs
Construct EP01; facet epistemic-method. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemology/)
Evidence: EPI001@1, EPI023@1.
A revisable belief need not be weakly supported. Contextual certainty and fallibility are not treated as simple opposites.

### Observation carries weight against an attractive theory
Construct EP04; facet epistemic-method. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemology/)
Evidence: EPI003@1, EPI016@1.
This reports evidential practice, not the thesis that every justified belief must arise from observation.

### Relevant expert agreement can be evidence
Construct EP07; facet epistemic-method. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemology/)
Evidence: EPI005@2, EPI018@1.
Trust in relevant expertise does not imply technocratic rule, infallible experts or uncritical deference.

### A belief’s longevity can provide some credibility
Construct EP08; facet epistemic-method. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemology/); [The Psychology of Worldviews](https://doi.org/10.1037/1089-2680.8.1.3)
Evidence: EPI019@1, EPI030@1.
Not equivalent to politically conservative commitments or blanket obedience to tradition.

### Intuition can provide initial justification
Construct EP09; facet epistemic-method. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemology/)
Evidence: EPI011@1, EPI031@1.
Initial justification can be defeasible; it is not a claim that intuitions are infallible.

### Some justification rests on basic beliefs or experiences
Construct EP12; facet epistemic-justification. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemology/)
Evidence: EPI038@1, EPI042@1.
Choosing another account does not reject every foundational element. Basic beliefs need not be infallible.

### A coherent web helps ground justification
Construct EP12; facet epistemic-justification. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemology/)
Evidence: EPI039@1, EPI042@1.
This comparison is about justification, not truth being whatever a group agrees on.

### Reliable processes can justify beliefs
Construct EP12; facet epistemic-justification. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemology/)
Evidence: EPI040@1, EPI042@1.
Process reliability need not be known by the believer. This does not entail a specific theory of political authority.

### Successful science can support reality of unobservables
Construct EP13; facet epistemic-justification. [Scientific Realism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/scientific-realism/)
Evidence: EPI013@1, EPI033@1.
Scientific realism can be fallible; allowing successful false theories is not a disqualifier.

### Values can legitimately shape evidential decisions under uncertainty
Construct EP14; facet epistemic-justification. [Scientific Objectivity](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/scientific-objectivity/)
Evidence: EPI044@1, EPI045@1.
This does not imply evidence is arbitrary or that all value influence is justified.

### A complete physical account could fully explain experience
Construct MS01; facet consciousness. [Physicalism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/physicalism/)
Evidence: MSI001@2, MSI006@1.
An explanatory gap alone does not establish nonphysical substances or supernaturalism.

### Functional organization can determine mental states
Construct MS02; facet consciousness. [Functionalism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/functionalism/)
Evidence: MSI013@1, MSI017@1.
Functional roles and implementation are distinct questions. Other theory choices do not automatically refute all functional claims.

### Simple physical systems may have basic experience
Construct MS02; facet consciousness. [Panpsychism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/panpsychism/)
Evidence: MSI015@1, MSI017@1.
This is a limited panpsychism-related comparison, not a claim that every object thinks like a person.

### The self can be a continuing process
Construct MS04; facet personal-identity. [Personal Identity](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/identity-personal/)
Evidence: MSI003@1, MSI011@1.
A process view does not establish any particular religious tradition or deny continuity and responsibility.

### Mental content can partly depend on the environment
Construct MS06; facet personal-identity. [Externalism About the Mind](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/content-externalism/)
Evidence: MSI019@1, MSI018@1.
Content externalism differs from an extended cognitive system and does not imply group priority.

### Conscious reasoning can genuinely affect action
Construct AH10; facet agency-causes. [Free Will](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/freewill/); [Nadelhoffer et al. (2014): The Free Will Inventory](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24561311/)
Evidence: AHI005@2, AHI032@1.
Caused reasoning can still be reasoning. This does not require uncaused choices or reject unconscious influences.

### Unconscious processes can cause behavior
Construct AH11; facet agency-causes. [Free Will](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/freewill/); [The Psychology of Worldviews](https://doi.org/10.1037/1089-2680.8.1.3)
Evidence: AHI011@1, AHI021@1.
Unconscious influence is not the thesis that conscious reasons never matter.

### Human life participates in purpose built into reality
Construct RC08; facet religious-claims. [The Meaning of Life](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/life-meaning/)
Evidence: RCI005@1, RCI008@1.
Does not establish objective moral requirements, a personal deity or felt meaning in someone’s life.

### Some genuinely supernatural reality exists
Construct RC03; facet religious-claims. [Naturalism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/naturalism/); [Atheism and Agnosticism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/atheism-agnosticism/)
Evidence: RCI003@2, RCI006@1.
An unexplained event is not by itself supernatural; this is an explicitly stated ontological commitment.

### Religious or spiritual commitments have personal importance
Construct RC10; facet religious-practice. [Atheism and Agnosticism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/atheism-agnosticism/)
Evidence: RCI013@1, RCI030@1.
Importance and divine existence are separate. Spiritual practice is not assigned a named religion.

### Sacred status can have independent normative weight
Construct RC09; facet religious-practice. [Ginges, Atran, Medin & Shikaki (2007), Sacred bounds on rational resolution of violent political conflict](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17460042/)
Evidence: RCI012@1, RCI029@1.
A secular person can value sacred things; neither religious law nor purity concerns automatically follow.

### Objective meaning need not require cosmic purpose
Construct EX01; facet existential-meaning. [The Meaning of Life](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/life-meaning/)
Evidence: EXI006@1, EXI011@1.
Objective meaning and self-created meaning can coexist. This does not infer optimism or religion.

### The world offers worthwhile possibilities
Construct EX06; facet existential-outlook. [Primal World Beliefs](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30299119/)
Evidence: EXI004@1, EXI017@1.
An original worldview-outlook comparison, not a Primal World Beliefs score or a mental-health assessment.

### Individuals have claims against group override
Construct SO01; facet social-persons. [Leif Wenar: Rights](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/rights/)
Evidence: SOI001@2, SOI025@1.
Not a denial of social dependence, group agency or the existence of institutions.

### Groups can meaningfully have goals
Construct SO02; facet social-entities. [Brian Epstein: Social Ontology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/social-ontology/)
Evidence: SOI002@2, SOI026@1.
Group agency is not the normative thesis that groups may override individual rights.

### Social practices partly constitute categories
Construct SO11; facet social-entities. [Brian Epstein: Social Ontology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/social-ontology/)
Evidence: SOI013@1, SOI036@1.
A practice-dependent category can still have real consequences. Not all social categories share one account.

### Consent is central to legitimate political coercion
Construct PL03; facet political-authority. [Fabienne Peter: Political Legitimacy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/legitimacy/); [Dagger & Lefkowitz: Political Obligation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/political-obligation/)
Evidence: PLI009@1, PLI056@1.
Explicit, tacit and hypothetical consent differ. This does not infer state abolition or a duty to obey all laws.

### Participation adds to political legitimacy
Construct PL04; facet political-authority. [Fabienne Peter: Political Legitimacy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/legitimacy/)
Evidence: PLI006@1, PLI027@1.
Democratic authorization is not a view about workplace ownership or a license to override all rights.

### Individual rights constrain aggregate benefit
Construct PL09; facet political-rights. [Leif Wenar: Rights](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/rights/); [Deontological Ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-deontological/)
Evidence: PLI008@1, PLI052@1.
A tradeoff about basic rights does not establish absolutism or a unique ground of rights.

### Legitimate acquisition can ground strong property claims
Construct PL11; facet political-economy. [Property and Ownership](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/property/)
Evidence: PLI013@1, PLI054@1.
Taking compensation into account does not by itself reject property. Acquisition, distribution and public use remain distinct.

### Desert can justify punishment without future benefit
Construct PL18; facet political-justice. [Legal Punishment](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/legal-punishment/)
Evidence: PLI017@1, PLI064@1.
Desert is distinct from deterrence and the permissibility of excessive punishment.

### Foreign interests merit consideration beyond national benefit
Construct PL22; facet political-scope. [Cosmopolitanism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/cosmopolitanism/)
Evidence: PLI020@1, PLI067@1.
This reports serious consideration for outsiders, not equal priority in every case. Giving compatriots priority does not by itself reject duties to outsiders.

### National membership can create special obligations
Construct PL23; facet political-scope. [Dagger & Lefkowitz: Political Obligation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/political-obligation/)
Evidence: PLI021@1, PLI068@1.
Special obligations need not imply superiority, hostility to foreigners or unlimited national authority.

### Multiple governing institutions can be valuable
Construct PL24; facet political-authority. [Elinor Ostrom (2010): Beyond Markets and States: Polycentric Governance of Complex Economic Systems](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.100.3.641)
Evidence: PLI022@1, PLI069@1.
Polycentric governance need not be stateless, and is not equivalent to abolishing courts.

### Religious authority can independently ground public law
Construct PL26; facet political-authority. [Fabienne Peter: Political Legitimacy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/legitimacy/); [Atheism and Agnosticism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/atheism-agnosticism/)
Evidence: PLI023@1, PLI070@1.
Private religious belief cannot substitute for this directly asked public-law commitment.

### Well-being can include goods not currently desired
Construct VA20; facet value-theories. [Well-Being](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/well-being/)
Evidence: VAI058@1, VAI054@1.
A plural answer does not specify which components count. Personal value priorities and normative ethics remain separate.

### Informed desire fulfillment can improve well-being
Construct VA20; facet value-theories. [Well-Being](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/well-being/)
Evidence: VAI054@1, VAI056@1.
Choosing another account is not blanket opposition to desire fulfillment. This does not identify ethical egoism.

### Aesthetic value can exceed actual liking
Construct VA21; facet value-theories. [Aesthetic Judgment](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aesthetic-judgment/)
Evidence: VAI061@1, VAI060@1.
This narrow aesthetic-objectivity comparison is not a claim about moral truth or a correct artistic taste.

## Academic upgrades

- Name the philosophical question, its alternatives and cross-loading risks.
- Record source URL, reviewed sections, date, access limits and whether evidence is conceptual or empirical.
- Map exact item revisions and exact response meanings; keep qualified alternatives and missingness distinct.
- Add positive, negative and counterexample regressions before activating an interpretation.
- Version the model and selection policy; material wording changes require a new item revision and bank/instrument release.
- Replay historical answers only against compatible bank versions; preserve the original snapshots.
- Test public form content coverage in addition to item counts, and verify the browser shows it.
- Treat study populations, language, incentives and response format as limits on empirical transfer.

- No cognitive-review gate is reinstated.
- A minimum of two authored evidence groups is an engineering display rule, not reliability or factor identification.
- Domain coverage is partial and largely Anglophone. No inference of Buddhist, Confucian, Hindu, Indigenous or other traditions from superficial similarity.
- The PhilPapers design is an organizational reference. Expert prevalence is not a truth key, classification prior or population norm.

The unchanged previous model is retained as generic-0.1.0. This release is generic-0.2.0. The generic runtime never supplies missing answers from philosophical names, demographics, memories or figures. Existing interface safeguards and post-completion game isolation remain.
